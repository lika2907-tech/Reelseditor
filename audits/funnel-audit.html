2. Does it attract a rich buyer?

Not yet. The page is written for a woman who cannot afford an editor. That is a real buyer, but she is not the one who later buys your coaching. Three lines do most of the damage:

  • "You would hire an editor, but $200 a reel is not in the budget yet." This is the pain line that selects your reader. It tells a woman with money that this page is not for her.
  • "The math: $2,400 vs $111." Pure cost-saving framing. Premium buyers do not buy to save $2,289. They buy to get time back, keep quality high and stay in control.
  • "All for $111. Once." The only price on the page is low, and there is no higher option. A woman who would happily pay $497 to have it set up for her has nowhere to put her money.

Add a premium path on the same page

Keep $111 as the entry, and add one higher option right beside it. This lifts average order value and signals quality to the wealthier buyer.

  • Order bump at checkout (around $47): "Signature Style Pack", 3 extra styles pre-tuned in your brand fonts and colors.
  • Premium tier ($497, capped): "Done-with-you install", a 45-minute call where you set it up together, tune her style and schedule her first week. Cap it at a number you can actually deliver each month.
  • Ascension: nothing currently points a buyer toward She Who Scales coaching. The delivery page and post-purchase emails are where that happens.

3. Does it create urgency?

No. The only urgency signal is the top bar: "Founding release $111". It has no end date, no cap and no next price, so a buyer reads it as the permanent price and bookmarks the page. Urgency must be real, or it becomes a trust problem.

LeverHow to use it honestly
Price deadline"Founding price $111 until [date]. Then $197." Put the date in the top bar, the pricing card and the sticky buy bar. Actually raise it.
Quantity cap"First 100 founding members" with a live counter, or cap the $497 done-with-you seats at 10 a month.
Founding-only bonusSomething that disappears after the window: a live group setup session, or your personal style template.
Cost of waiting"Every week you wait is 7 reels that stay in your camera roll." Ties urgency to her pain, not to your launch.
Abandon cart emails3 emails over 48 hours, the last one sent before the price goes up. Needs email captured first.

6. Smaller notes

  • Check the "it posts for you" claim. Auto-posting to Instagram usually needs a Business or Creator account and a scheduler connection. Say what is needed in the FAQ so nobody feels misled.
  • Mention the real running cost. A paid Claude or ChatGPT plan (about $20 a month) is listed, which is good. Say it again in the pricing card so the $111 "once" promise stays honest.
  • "Lifetime updates" is a long promise for a tool that depends on outside AI platforms. Consider "every update for as long as the engine is sold".
  • Earnings disclaimer is not needed now. Add one if you add income claims with real user results.

Priority order

  1. Connect checkout, with email captured first.
  2. Remove the demo note, record the demo, add real testimonials, write the refund policy.
  3. Add a dated founding deadline and the next price everywhere the price appears.
  4. Rewrite the pain section and the math block for the rich buyer.
  5. Add the order bump and the $497 done-with-you tier.
  6. Build the checkout, delivery page, abandon cart emails and nurture, in that order.
